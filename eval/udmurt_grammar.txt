# Udmurt Language: Morphological and Syntactic Grammar Resource
# For Ontological Disambiguation of Morphological Analyses
#
# This document describes the morphological and syntactic properties of the
# Udmurt language as used in the UniParser-udm annotation system.
# Tag notation follows the UniParser-udm tagset (Arkhangelskiy, 2016):
#   https://github.com/timarkh/uniparser-grammar-udm
#
# Purpose: to support morphological disambiguation of ambiguous token analyses
# produced by UniParser. Each section describes linguistic categories, their
# features, and constraints that help select the correct analysis when a token
# has multiple candidates separated by '||' in parser output.


# =============================================================================
# 1. TYPOLOGICAL OVERVIEW
# =============================================================================

Udmurt is an agglutinative Uralic language of the Permic branch, spoken in
the Udmurt Republic of Russia. It has SOV (Subject-Object-Verb) basic word
order, though word order is relatively free due to rich morphological marking.
Udmurt is a head-final language: postpositions follow their nouns, and
subordinate clauses precede their matrix clause.

Morphological categories are expressed by suffixes that attach to a stem (STEM).
Every analyzed token in the corpus begins with STEM in the tag sequence,
followed by morphological suffixes separated by hyphens: STEM-SUFFIX1-SUFFIX2.

Udmurt lacks grammatical gender. Number is marked on nouns and verbs.
There is no definite/indefinite article.


# =============================================================================
# 2. PART-OF-SPEECH CATEGORIES AND THEIR DIAGNOSTIC SUFFIXES
# =============================================================================

2.1 VERBS

Verbs in Udmurt are identified by tense/mood/person suffixes.

A token bears a verbal analysis when its tag contains any of the following:
- Tense markers: PST (past), PRS (present), FUT (future)
- Mood markers: IMP (imperative), DEB (debitive/necessitative)
- Verbal noun suffix: VN (-он/-ён: action nominalizations)
- Converb suffix: CVB (-са: simultaneous or sequential action)
- Infinitive suffix: INF (-ыны/-ини)
- Participle suffixes: PTCP.ACT (active, -ись), PTCP.PST (past passive, -эм/-ем)
- Evidential: EVID (-эм/-ем when used as a mirative/inferential past)

IMPORTANT DISAMBIGUATION NOTE (verbs vs. nouns):
The suffix -эм/-ем is AMBIGUOUS between:
  (a) PTCP.PST (past passive participle, modifying a noun: "done X", "made X")
  (b) EVID (evidential past tense: speaker did not witness the event)
  (c) STEM alone if the form is a lexicalized noun
Context rule: if the token precedes a noun and modifies it, prefer PTCP.PST.
If the token is clause-final and the clause lacks an auxiliary, prefer EVID.
If the token is accompanied by a copula (луыны), prefer PTCP.PST-LOC (result state).

2.2 NOUNS AND NOMINAL FORMS

Nouns are identified by case suffixes or by bare STEM (nominative).
A token has a nominal analysis when its tag contains:
- Case suffixes: GEN, DAT, ACC, ILL, LOC, EL, ABL, INS, ADV, PROL, DELIM
- Plural suffix: PL
- Possessive suffixes: P.1SG, P.2SG, P.3SG, P.1PL, P.2PL, P.3PL
- Ordinal: ORD (for numerals: -этӥ/-тӥ)
- Comparative: COMP (-гес)
- Attributive: ATTR (-о/-ё)
- Bare STEM without verbal suffixes = nominative singular noun or uninflected form

2.3 POSTPOSITIONS

Postpositions in Udmurt often derive from nouns with locative cases.
They govern specific cases on their nominal complements.
Disambiguation: a form tagged both as STEM (noun) and STEM-INS (postposition)
should be resolved by checking whether a nominal complement precedes it.
Example: "сэрен" can be STEM (adverb 'because') or STEM-INS (postposition 'because of').
If a genitive-marked noun precedes it, prefer the postpositional reading.

2.4 PARTICLES, CONJUNCTIONS, ADVERBS

Words tagged as bare STEM with no inflectional suffixes are typically:
- Adverbs: татын (here), отын (there), али (now), ни (already), уката (more)
- Conjunctions: но (and/also), нош (but/and), ук (emphatic), дыр (evidential particle)
- Particles: ке (if/conditional), ӝик (comparative particle)
These are uninflected and do not participate in agreement or government.
Their analysis is always unambiguous (single STEM tag).


# =============================================================================
# 3. VERBAL MORPHOLOGY AND DISAMBIGUATION RULES
# =============================================================================

3.1 TENSE SYSTEM

Present tense (PRS):
  - PRS.3SG: 3rd person singular present, suffix -э/-е
  - PRS.3PL: 3rd person plural present, suffix -о
  - PRS.12: covers both 1st and 2nd person present (polypersonal form), suffix -ськ-о/-ськ-е
  Tag PRS.12 appears when the form is ambiguous between 1SG and 2SG present.

Past tense (PST):
  - PST-3SG: 3rd person singular past, suffix -и-з / -(э)з
  - PST-3PL: 3rd person plural past, suffix -и-зы
  - PST-1PL: 1st person plural past, suffix -и-м
  - PST-1SG: 1st person singular past, suffix -и

Future tense (FUT):
  - FUT-3SG: 3rd person singular future, suffix -о-з
  - FUT-3PL: 3rd person plural future, suffix -о-зы
  Future and present plural forms are sometimes homophonous.
  DISAMBIGUATION: FUT-3SG (-оз) vs. PRS.3SG (-э) are distinct phonologically.
  FUT-3PL (-озы) vs. PRS.3PL (-о) disambiguation depends on the suffix vowel.
  In context, future is preferred in purposive clauses with infinitives and
  in clauses expressing scheduled or intended events.

Debitive mood (DEB):
  The debitive expresses obligation or necessity: "must, should, ought to".
  Tag: STEM-DEB, suffix -оно/-ёно.
  The debitive form is a verbal noun used predicatively.
  DISAMBIGUATION: DEB forms often co-occur with a copula (луыны, луоз),
  but can appear as predicates alone. If the token ends in -оно and functions
  as the main predicate of a clause, prefer DEB over any nominal reading.

Imperative mood (IMP):
  - IMP.SG: 2nd person singular imperative (bare stem or stem with -э/-е)
  - IMP.PL: 2nd person plural imperative, suffix -э/-е + plural

CRITICAL DISAMBIGUATION: STEM-PST-3SG vs. STEM-IMP.PL
  The 2nd plural imperative form and certain past 3SG forms can be homophonous.
  Rule: In declarative affirmative clauses with an expressed subject (3rd person
  noun or pronoun), prefer PST-3SG. Imperative is only appropriate in
  commands/requests addressed to 2nd person interlocutors, typically in
  clause-initial position without an overt 3rd-person subject.
  Example: "Революция кутскиз" → subject "революция" (3SG noun) forces PST-3SG,
  not IMP.PL.

3.2 VOICE: PASSIVE AND CAUSATIVE

Passive voice (PASS):
  The passive is formed with the suffix -ськ- (-sk-) inserted between the
  stem and personal endings.
  Tag notation: STEM-PASS-TENSE-PERSON (e.g., STEM-PASS-PST-3SG)
  The passive construal requires that the notional agent is demoted (absent or
  in instrumental case). The surface subject is the notional patient.

CRITICAL DISAMBIGUATION: STEM-PST-3SG vs. STEM-PASS-PST-3SG
  Both share the same personal ending -з (3SG past).
  Selection rule:
  1. If the subject NP is a typical agent (animate, volitional) and the verb
     is transitive, prefer the active STEM-PST-3SG analysis.
  2. If the verb stem is inherently intransitive (e.g., кутскыны 'to begin',
     потыны 'to come out'), the passive reading is semantically anomalous —
     prefer the active reading STEM-PST-3SG.
  3. If the verb stem is transitive (e.g., кутыны 'to take', верыны 'to say')
     AND the surface subject is patientive (inanimate or theme-role), prefer
     STEM-PASS.
  4. The suffix -ськ- also derives middle/reflexive/intransitive verbs from
     transitive bases. These should be treated as lexically intransitive and
     their personal endings parsed as active voice.

Causative voice (CAUS):
  The causative is formed with suffix -т-/-ыт- between stem and endings.
  Tag: STEM-CAUS-TENSE-PERSON
  The causative implies that the subject causes another agent to perform the action.
  Disambiguation: CAUS forms are rarer; prefer CAUS only when a causer-causee
  relationship is semantically plausible.

3.3 ITERATIVE ASPECT

The iterative suffix -ыл-/-ул- marks repeated or habitual actions.
Tag: STEM-ITER-TENSE-PERSON
The iterative is unambiguous in most contexts. When a form has both iterative
and non-iterative candidates, prefer iterative if contextual adverbs of
frequency are present (уно 'many times', котькуд 'every', пол 'time/occasion').

3.4 NON-FINITE VERB FORMS

Infinitive (INF): suffix -ыны/-ини
  Used in complement clauses of modal verbs and in purposive constructions.
  Infinitives follow the governing verb: быгатыны (to be able) + INF.
  DISAMBIGUATION: If the token ends in -ыны and follows a modal or control verb,
  prefer INF over any other reading.

Converb (CVB): suffix -са
  Expresses simultaneous or immediately preceding action relative to the
  main verb. The converb shares its subject with the main verb.
  If a token ends in -са and a finite verb follows in the same clause,
  prefer CVB.

Active participle (PTCP.ACT): suffix -ись/-ысь
  Functions as an adjectival modifier (pre-nominal) or as a nominalized agent.
  DISAMBIGUATION: PTCP.ACT vs. bare STEM (nouns in -ись):
  If the token modifies a following noun, prefer PTCP.ACT.
  If the token is in argument position without a modified noun, it may be
  a lexicalized agent noun (nominalization); treat as Noun.

Past passive participle (PTCP.PST): suffix -эм/-ем
  Functions as an adjectival modifier expressing a completed action/state.
  Also used in perfect/resultative periphrastic constructions with луыны.

Verbal noun (VN): suffix -он/-ён
  Nominalizes the verbal action. Takes case suffixes: STEM-VN-DAT, STEM-VN-ADV.
  Functions as a noun in argument positions or in adverbial constructions.
  STEM-VN-ADV (adverbial verbal noun): expresses manner or circumstance.


# =============================================================================
# 4. NOMINAL MORPHOLOGY AND CASE SYSTEM
# =============================================================================

4.1 CASE INVENTORY

Udmurt has 15 cases. The cases relevant to disambiguation in the corpus are:

Nominative: unmarked (bare STEM). Subject of a finite clause.
Genitive (GEN): suffix -лэн/-лэсь/-лэн; possessor or complement of postpositions.
  NOTE: GEN and ABL share the same marker -лэсь in some analyses.
Dative (DAT): suffix -лы; indirect object, beneficiary, goal.
Accusative/Illative (ACC/ILL): suffix -эз/-ез (singular object), -ты/-тэ (plural).
  The ACC and ILL are HOMOPHONOUS in many forms.
  DISAMBIGUATION: ACC marks the direct object of a transitive verb.
  ILL marks motion toward a location (into, to).
  Rule: If the governing verb is a verb of motion (вуыны 'arrive', мыныны 'go',
  лыктыны 'come'), prefer ILL. If the governing verb is transitive and takes
  a patient, prefer ACC.
Illative (ILL): suffix -э/-е (locative-directional: 'into, to').
  Distinct from ACC-ILL in that it marks only directional semantics.
Locative/Inessive (LOC): suffix -ын; location ('in, at, on').
Elative (EL): suffix -ысь; source location ('from, out of').
Ablative (ABL): suffix -лэсь; source of comparison, ablative of separation.
Instrumental (INS): suffix -эн/-ен; instrument, comitative, means.
Adverbial (ADV): suffix -я; manner, domain, topic ('about', 'according to').
Prolative (PROL): suffix -этӥ/-тӥ; path ('along, through').
Approximative: suffix -лань; direction ('toward').
Egressive: suffix -ысен; starting point in time/space.
Caritive: suffix -тэк; absence ('without').
Terminative: suffix -озь; limit ('until, up to').
Delimitative (DELIM): suffix -джык; approximative or delimitative reading.

4.2 POSSESSIVE SUFFIXES

Possessive suffixes attach after case suffixes:
  P.1SG (-е/-й): my
  P.2SG (-эд/-ед): your (sg)
  P.3SG (-эз/-ез): his/her/its — MOST COMMON, FREQUENT SOURCE OF AMBIGUITY
  P.1PL (-мы): our
  P.2PL (-ды): your (pl)
  P.3PL (-зы): their

DISAMBIGUATION: P.3SG suffix -эз/-ез is homophonous with ACC/ILL object suffix.
  Rule: If the head noun has a genitive-marked possessor preceding it, prefer P.3SG.
  If the noun is in direct object position with a transitive verb and no
  possessor is expressed, prefer ACC.

4.3 PLURAL

Plural suffix -ос/-ёс (PL) attaches before case and possessive suffixes.
Plural is unambiguous except when combined with other suffixes that may create
homophony.


# =============================================================================
# 5. AGREEMENT AND GOVERNMENT
# =============================================================================

5.1 SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT

In Udmurt, finite verbs agree with their subject in person and number.

Agreement rules:
- A 3rd person singular subject (noun phrase without plural marking) requires
  a 3SG verb form: PST-3SG, PRS.3SG, or FUT-3SG.
- A 3rd person plural subject (noun with PL suffix or plural pronoun) requires
  a 3PL verb form: PST-3PL, PRS.3PL, or FUT-3PL.
- A 1st or 2nd person pronoun (мон 'I', тон 'you', ми 'we', тӥ 'you.PL')
  requires the corresponding person agreement on the verb.
- The special tag PRS.12 covers forms ambiguous between 1SG and 2SG.
  If the subject is мон (1SG), prefer the interpretation as 1SG present.
  If the subject is тон (2SG), prefer 2SG present.
  If no overt subject is present, the form is genuinely ambiguous and
  PRS.12 is the appropriate analysis.

CRITICAL RULE: Subject person constrains verb person.
  If a 3rd person nominal subject is present (not мон, тон, ми, тӥ),
  any candidate analysis with PRS.12 or any 1st/2nd person form should be
  rejected in favor of a 3rd person form if one exists among the candidates.

5.2 POSTPOSITIONAL GOVERNMENT

Postpositions in Udmurt govern specific cases:
- вылын (on, locative): governs Genitive (STEM-GEN)
- вылэ (onto, illative): governs Genitive
- улын (under, locative): governs Genitive
- борды (to, toward): governs Dative
- сэрен (because of): governs Genitive or appears as adverb (no governed NP)
- ваме (in front of): governs Genitive
- бордын (near, at): governs Genitive
- пала (toward, about): governs Genitive

DISAMBIGUATION: A form that can be analyzed both as a STEM (intransitive noun)
and as a postposition (STEM-INS or similar) should be resolved by checking
whether a governed nominal phrase precedes it.
Rule: If a genitive-marked nominal precedes the candidate postposition,
prefer the postpositional reading.

5.3 CASE GOVERNMENT BY VERBS

Transitive verbs govern the accusative/illative on their direct object.
Verbs of motion govern the illative on their goal argument.
The negative auxiliary (уд 'NEG.2SG', ум 'NEG.1SG/PL', уг 'NEG.3SG') governs
a special negated verb form (NEG.SG or NEG.PL).

NEGATION DISAMBIGUATION:
  Negative finite forms end in -ы (NEG.SG) or alternate forms.
  Tag: STEM-NEG.SG or STEM-NEG.PL.
  The negative auxiliary and the negated lexical verb together constitute
  the verbal predicate. The lexical verb under negation loses its own
  tense/person marking; those are encoded on the negative auxiliary.
  Rule: A form tagged STEM-NEG.SG/PL is only valid in a clause that also
  contains one of the negative auxiliaries: уд, ум, уг, ӧд, ӧм, ӧз.


# =============================================================================
# 6. SPECIFIC DISAMBIGUATION RULES FOR COMMON AMBIGUITIES
# =============================================================================

6.1 PST-3SG vs. PASS-PST-3SG (most frequent ambiguity in corpus)

The past 3SG active form and the passive past 3SG are formally identical
except for the presence of the passive suffix -ськ- in the passive stem.
However, many verbs in -ськ- are lexically intransitive (decausative/middle),
not passivized forms of transitive verbs.

Decision procedure:
  Step 1: Is the verb stem inherently intransitive (motion, change-of-state,
          inception verbs like кутскыны 'begin', потыны 'exit', луыны 'become')?
          → Prefer active analysis STEM-PST-3SG.
  Step 2: Is the subject animate and volitional?
          → Prefer active analysis STEM-PST-3SG.
  Step 3: Is the verb stem a transitive verb (transfer, creation, perception)?
          AND is the surface subject inanimate or theme-role?
          → Prefer passive analysis STEM-PASS-PST-3SG.
  Step 4: If undecidable from context, mark as unresolved.

6.2 STEM-PRS.12 vs. STEM-PASS-PRS.3PL vs. STEM-PASS-FUT (three-way ambiguity)

This is the hardest case in the corpus (see: "уллясько").
  - PRS.12: active present, 1st or 2nd person subject
  - PASS-PRS.3PL: passive present, 3rd person plural subject
  - PASS-FUT: passive future (no person specification in this tag)

Decision procedure:
  Step 1: Is there an overt 1st or 2nd person subject pronoun (мон, тон, ми, тӥ)?
          → Prefer PRS.12.
  Step 2: Is there an overt 3rd person plural subject?
          → Prefer PASS-PRS.3PL.
  Step 3: Is the clause expressing a future/generic state without a specific agent?
          → Consider PASS-FUT.
  Step 4: Is the verb used with 1st/2nd person context (narrative voice, direct speech)?
          → Prefer PRS.12.
  Step 5: If the sentence contains мон (I) as subject, prefer PRS.12 → read as 1SG.
  Default: In newspaper texts with no overt subject, PRS.12 is the most frequent
           reading for -сько forms.

6.3 STEM-PRS.3PL vs. STEM-FUT vs. STEM-ATTR (three-way ambiguity in -о forms)

Many verb forms ending in -о are ambiguous:
  - PRS.3PL: present tense 3rd plural (сюро 'there are')
  - FUT: future tense (without person/number specification in some analyses)
  - ATTR: attributive/adjectival form ending in -о/-ё

Decision procedure:
  Step 1: Is the token modifying a following noun?
          → Prefer ATTR (attributive).
  Step 2: Is there an overt 3rd plural subject?
          → Prefer PRS.3PL.
  Step 3: Is the clause expressing prospective or predicted events?
          → Prefer FUT.

6.4 PTCP.PST vs. EVID (ambiguity of -эм/-ем forms)

The suffix -эм/-ем is the most ambiguous suffix in Udmurt:
  - PTCP.PST: participial modifier ('done', 'made'), pre-nominal
  - EVID: evidential/inferential past, the speaker did not witness the event

Decision procedure:
  Step 1: Does the token immediately precede a noun in a genitive/possessive
          construction or as a modifier?
          → Prefer PTCP.PST.
  Step 2: Is the token clause-final or in predicate position?
          → Prefer EVID.
  Step 3: Is the clause reporting second-hand information, folklore, or
          surprising facts?
          → Prefer EVID.

6.5 SOИН (INS case) vs. СОИН (pronoun + postposition)

The form "соин" (with capital С: "Соин") can be:
  - STEM-INS: instrumental form of pronoun со 'that/it' ('with it/that')
  - STEM || STEM-INS: two tokens — со (pronoun) + ин (postposition?) or
    adverb со-ин (therefore)
  
  In the corpus tag STEM || STEM-INS means the token is ambiguous between
  these readings. Decision: if the token functions as a sentence connective
  ('therefore, for that reason'), prefer the adverb reading STEM.


# =============================================================================
# 7. SENTENCE-LEVEL CONTEXT RULES
# =============================================================================

7.1 CLAUSE TYPE DETECTION

Declarative finite clauses:
  A clause is declarative if it contains no interrogative pronoun (кин 'who',
  ма 'what', кытын 'where') and no question particle.
  In declarative clauses, indicative tense forms are preferred over
  imperative forms.

Question clauses:
  Interrogative pronouns кин (who), ма (what), кытын (where), кыӵе (what kind)
  mark the clause as interrogative. Verbal predicates in questions use
  the same indicative forms as declarative clauses.

Negative clauses:
  Presence of negative auxiliaries (уд, ум, уг, ӧд, ӧм, ӧз) marks the clause
  as negative. The lexical verb following the negative auxiliary takes NEG form.

Imperative clauses:
  An imperative clause typically lacks an overt subject and is clause-initial
  with the verb. In text genre (newspaper), imperatives are rare.
  RULE: Do not prefer imperative analysis unless the clause has no overt
  3rd-person subject and has imperative-compatible structure.

7.2 GENRE CONSTRAINTS

The corpus consists of newspaper texts (press genre, years 2008–2017).
Newspaper texts have the following statistical tendencies:
  - Third person forms (PST-3SG, FUT-3SG, PRS.3SG) are much more frequent
    than 1st/2nd person forms.
  - PRS.12 occurs primarily in quoted speech.
  - Passive forms occur in formal/official register passages.
  - Evidential (EVID) forms occur in reporting clauses.
  - Debitive (DEB) forms occur in planning or obligation statements.

When all other constraints are inconclusive, prefer 3rd person active
indicative readings over 1st/2nd person or passive readings in newspaper text.

7.3 COORDINATION AND ELLIPSIS

In coordinated verbal predicates (joined by но 'and'), all coordinated verbs
share the same tense, person, and number as the first conjunct.
Rule: If a token has candidate analyses of different tense/person, and a
coordinated verb with clear tense/person marking precedes it, prefer the
analysis that matches the preceding verb's features.

# 9. EXAMPLES OF DISAMBIGUATION FROM THE CORPUS
# =============================================================================

Example 1: "Революция кутскиз."
  Token: кутскиз
  Candidates: STEM-PST-3SG || STEM-PASS-PST-3SG
  Lemma candidates: кутскыны (to begin) || кутыны (to take/seize)
  Resolution: STEM-PST-3SG
  Reasoning: кутскыны is inherently intransitive (inchoative verb 'to start').
  The passive reading (кутыны + PASS) is semantically anomalous here because
  the subject "Революция" is an event noun that cannot volitionally 'take' anything.
  Agreement: "Революция" is 3SG nominal → 3SG verb form is required → PST-3SG confirmed.

Example 2: "Уллясько, нош со уг но вырӟы."
  Token: уллясько
  Candidates: STEM-PRS.12 || STEM-PASS-PRS.3PL || STEM-PASS-FUT
  Subject: first-person implied (narrative voice, direct speech context)
  Resolution: STEM-PRS.12
  Reasoning: No 3rd person plural subject present. The sentence structure and
  the presence of со 'it' as a separate subject (for вырӟы) indicates the
  first clause has a 1st person subject (dropped). Prefer PRS.12.

Example 3: "Туннэ нуналлы татын сюро таза пудолэн лыдыз 900 йыр."
  Token: сюро
  Candidates: STEM-PRS.3PL || STEM-FUT || STEM-ATTR
  Context: "today there are 900 healthy livestock"
  Resolution: STEM-PRS.3PL
  Reasoning: "туннэ" (today) forces present tense reading. The subject "лыдыз"
  (their number) is 3SG, but the numeric predicate construction triggers 3PL agreement.
  ATTR is excluded because сюро is not followed by a noun it modifies here.

Example 4: "Соин сэрен ужась комбайнъёс сылоно луылӥзы."
  Token: соин
  Candidates: STEM || STEM-INS
  Resolution: depends on syntactic role — adverb reading (STEM) is preferred
  when the token functions as a sentence connective ('therefore').
  Token: ужась
  Candidates: STEM-PTCP.ACT || STEM (bare stem noun 'worker')
  Following token: комбайнъёс (harvesters, plural noun)
  Resolution: STEM-PTCP.ACT — the token precedes a noun and modifies it
  ('working harvesters'). Prefer participial reading.
